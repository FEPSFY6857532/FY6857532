Abstract

Lots of video games highly rely on real-world physics from the most complex equations you can think of to the most fundamental laws. A very prevalent law of physics used in video games are Newton’s laws of motion. This is used at such a high rate to ensure the movement of the characters are not only realistic but also make sense. After compiling all the algorithms needed, video game developers must attempt to ensure their game is entertaining for the user. However, for more advanced games, these movements are not enough, therefore geometric shapes are used to control the limbs of in-game characters to enhance the player’s experience. This is used a lot more in realistic video games to simulate human characteristics for more realistic elements. The reason why all this detail which is based on basic elements of physics is for users to be entertained and escape from reality. Without the basic laws of physics, video games would not work, and players would not experience any type of pleasure due to this.

Providing a Good Gaming Experience Now we all have these algorithms implemented but then we must use all of this to then provide a good gaming experience for players. Consistency is crucial for keeping players immersed in game environments; it’s ok to be able to run 100mph and jump 20 feet – control the rest of the world reacts appropriately (Hecker, C. (2000)). Designers must be able to tell a dramatic story with its natural twists and turns when everything in the game is controlled by physical laws (Hecker, C. (2000)). This is why we use physical control. This is about knowing which forces need to be computed during the force-computation stage (Hecker, C.(2000)). By doing this we are able to simulate the motions in-game accurate to what it would be like in real life. Furthermore, in video games humans are vital whether it is shooting games or sports games humans are at the epicentre of it. This is why due to physical control, it involves a very simple physical model for each character, as in the box and sphere approximations mentioned earlier, and the physical model moves around the world while the character’s graphical representation animates (Hecker, C. (2000)) . The character’s limb movements do not affect the physical simulation in any way at all; such prescripted animations are usually either hand-animated or motion captured from a human performer (Hecker, C. (2000)). Many video game developers use this method as they try to apply the laws of physics, the game developer breaks down each physical object in the game into a collection of simple geometric components—cubes, spheres, or cylinders—connected by joints (Sincell,M. (1999)). As technology has advanced this has been a more common practice especially in sports games as they aim to create a realistic feel to the game (such as NBA 2k and all their dribbling, shooting and dunking animations). By doing this, it gives a clearer gaming experience to apply the real physics from the real world and have the ability to give the best possible gaming experience.
