Abstract

Lots of video games highly rely on real-world physics which can involve the most complex equations or something as simple as a fundamental law. A very prevalent law of physics used in video games are Newton’s laws of motion. This is used at such a high rate to ensure the movement of the characters are realistic and make sense. After compiling all the algorithms needed, video game developers must attempt to ensure their game is entertaining for the user which means that the movement functions of the game must be correct (this is where Newton's laws of motion come in). However, for more advanced games, these programmes are not enough, therefore geometric shapes are used to control the limbs of in-game characters to enhance the realism of the game. This is used a lot more in modern video games to simulate human characteristics and movement which ensures smooth gameplay. The reason why all this detail (based on basic elements of physics) is for users to be entertained and simulate closer to reality stories on screen where the user can have full control of. It is like being in control of your own movie. Whether that could be playing their favourite superhero character or reliving theior childhood dreams of being a football player, video games is a good way of showcasing this. Without the basic laws of physics, video games would not work and players would not enjoy these experiences.

Newton’s Laws in Physics

Computer games are all about movement and the new generation of programmers use physical laws to create objects that obey a specific set of rules in all circumstances (Sincell,M. 1999). A clear example of this are Newton’s 3 laws of motion. This is abundant in popular games, from gravitational forces in Angry Birds to dynamics in car racing games (Neumann, M. D., & Dion, L. (2021)). The mechanics of this is then divided into two parts kinematics and dimensions (Neumann, M. D., & Dion, L. (2021)). Now, in Newton’s first law of motion it states (on the kinematics side) an object at rest stays at rest until an outside force acts on it and that an object in motion stays in motion in a straight line at a constant speed until acted on by an outside force. Therefore, a force has a push or pull effect on an object (Giancoli 2005, 72). To simulate this law a code must be put in. The glide function is what shows the object slowing down due to a force (which is frictional in this case). This demonstartion is shown on the video below. Now in every single game there are collisions, a famous game such as FIFA has collisions all the time including the players and the football. The key understanding of this is through Newton’s 3rd law of motion.

Using Newton's laws is a basic example of how physics are implemented in video games. As mentioned previosuly, the glide function is a key example of how an object accelerates and decelerates to show how an object were to move in real-life due to a collison. However, in video games lots of different functions and codes are all to intertwined and a key example of this is physical control (Hecker, C. (2000)).

Providing a Good Gaming Experience

Now we all have these algorithms implemented but then we must use all of this to then provide a good gaming experience for players. Consistency is crucial for keeping players immersed in game environments; it’s ok to be able to run 100mph and jump 20 feet but you also need to control what the rest of the virtual world reacts appropriately (Hecker, C. (2000)). Designers must be able to tell a dramatic story with its natural twists when everything in the game is controlled by physical laws (Hecker, C. (2000)). This is why we use physical control. This is about knowing which forces need to be computed during the force-computation stage (Hecker, C.(2000)). By doing this we are able to simulate the motions in-game accurate to what it would be like in real life. Furthermore, in video games humans are vital whether it is shooting games or sports games, humans are at the epicentre of it. This is why due to physical control, it involves a very simple physical model for each character and how it moves around the virtual world while the character’s graphical representation animates (Hecker, C. (2000)) . The character’s limb movements do not affect the physical simulation in any way at all; such prescripted animations are usually either hand-animated or motion captured from a human performer (Hecker, C. (2000)). Many video game developers use this method (using laws of physics) in order for the game developer to break down each physical object in the game into a collection of simple geometric components—cubes, spheres, or cylinders—connected by joints (Sincell,M. (1999)). This relates to the laws of physics as they are using real-life physical movements based on physics to be implemented in the game. As technology has advanced, this has been a more common practice especially in sports games as they aim to create a realistic feeling (such as NBA 2k and all their dribbling, shooting and dunking animations).
